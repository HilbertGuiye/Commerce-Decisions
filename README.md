# Consumer & Financial Decisions
## A guide for players

You have been selected for your school football team. Your first match is in Week 6, but you need football boots before you can play. The boots cost $300, and the school will contribute $100. Can you save the remaining **$200** while managing other things you want or need?

In this interactive Commerce activity, you will make choices about earning, saving and spending money. Each choice affects your balance and your final outcome. Be ready to explain your priorities and what you give up along the way.

## Getting started

1. Open the game link provided by your teacher. If you have downloaded the game, fully unzip the folder and open `index.html`.
2. Read the introduction, then click the buttons or underlined choices **inside the picture**.
3. Follow the story and complete any worksheet tasks your teacher has set.

For a downloaded copy, keep the game files and `assets` folder together. You do not need PowerPoint or an account to play.

## How to play

- **Choose an income strategy.** You can save your weekly money, apply for a school job, sell items, or combine the options offered.
- **Read before choosing.** Compare the pay, time commitments, costs and repair conditions shown in the story.
- **Select items to sell.** Click an item to select it; click it again to remove it. You may select more than one item.
- **Check your budget.** At the Week 3 and Week 6 checkpoints, calculate your balance and show your working.
- **Make spending decisions.** Think about how buying GTA VI or repairing your phone will affect the money available for your football goal.
- **Review your ending.** Look at the consequences of your choices, then discuss whether you would make the same decisions again.

All prices and payments belong to the game's fictional scenario. Use the amounts shown in the activity for your calculations.

### Calculation checkpoints

Enter numbers in the answer boxes, then choose **Check my calculation** for feedback. If an amount is highlighted, review your income and spending, correct your answer and try again.

Use **Review my choices** to see the information needed for your calculation. If you are working on a paper worksheet, complete your working there and select the worksheet option when continuing. Follow your teacher's instructions about recording or submitting your work.

## Useful controls

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Your choices** | Shows your current job, items sold and spending choices. |
| **Reading view** | Displays the story and options in a layout that is easier to read on a small screen. |
| **Fullscreen** | Expands the game where your browser supports it. |
| **Restart** | Lets you begin again with new choices. Confirming a restart clears your current game. |
| **Music** and **Sound** | Turn background music and sound effects on or off separately. |
| **Volume sliders** | Adjust music and sound-effect volume independently. |

Music begins after your first game-button click, or when you choose **Music: Start**. You can play with both music and sound effects switched off.

For keyboard navigation, use **Tab** to move between controls and **Enter** or **Space** to activate buttons.

Your progress is kept in the current browser tab so you can refresh and continue. Keep that tab open while completing the activity.

## Think about your decisions

At the end, discuss or record your answers:

1. Did you reach your football goal? Which decision had the greatest effect?
2. What did you treat as a need, and what did you treat as a want?
3. What was the opportunity cost of one of your choices—what did you give up?
4. How did price, quality and warranty affect your phone-repair decision?
5. If you played again, what would you change, and why?

Different priorities can lead to different outcomes. Use your calculations and the story to support your explanation.
